Notes

Tables: click any column header to sort by it; click again to flip the order.

Quarterbacks tab: the Total chart includes every player with enough yards, at any position.

Running backs: players whose most-listed position is RB. YPC = rushing yards per carry. Two players who share a name show their rookie year, e.g. Adrian Peterson (2007).

Wide receivers: players whose most-listed position is WR (tight ends aren't included). Y/R = receiving yards per catch.

Tight ends: players whose most-listed position is TE; receiving stats only. Y/R = receiving yards per catch.

Wild card: the first playoff week of each season. "No WC" drops those games (divisional, conference and Super Bowl games remain). Wild-card games exist from 1978 on.

Slider: A retired player whose career was shorter than N counts with his full career.

* means the player is still active and his window includes the current 2026 season (or one he hasn't reached yet), so his totals will keep growing.

How the data is sourced

  • 1999 to today: game-by-game player stats from nflverse, an open-source project built from the NFL's official game data. Regular season and playoffs; preseason is excluded. A GitHub Action rebuilds the data every morning and republishes the site whenever new games have been played (currently through Week 10 of 2026).
  • 1950–1998: game-by-game stats (NFL, plus the AFL from 1960–69, which NFL.com counts in official careers), regular season and playoffs, from the NFL Football Player Stats dataset on Kaggle (game logs collected from Pro Football Reference in 2017). A game is a playoff game when it comes after that season's regular-season schedule (12 games through 1960 in the NFL, 14 in the AFL from 1960 and in both leagues from 1961, 16 from 1978, 9 in 1982 and 15 in 1987); the wild-card round is the first playoff weekend from 1978 on.
  • How it was checked: its regular seasons add up exactly to a second, independent source (fantasydatapros) for 99.9% of 1970–1998 player-seasons (it doesn't cover earlier years) (the rest are two players who share a name). Where it overlaps nflverse (1999–2016), about 99% of regular-season and 99.9% of playoff player-seasons match exactly. Against NFL.com, every regular season checked matches for Johnny Unitas, Jim Brown, Joe Namath, Gale Sayers, Dan Marino, Emmitt Smith and Jerry Rice, as do individual playoff games for Bart Starr (Super Bowl I season), Namath (Super Bowl III season), Marino (1984), Joe Montana (1989), Smith (1995) and Rice (1988). The site's build re-runs the fantasydatapros cross-check every time it updates.
  • Fixes: three regular-season games are missing from nflverse (1999 Rams–Ravens, 2000 Chiefs–Chargers and Bills–Dolphins); each affected player's missing game is filled in from his fantasydatapros season totals.
  • Players: Seasons count from the rookie year. From 1999, "games" counts games with at least one recorded stat; before 1999, games played. Passing yards are gross (sacks not subtracted). Two players with the same name show their rookie year, e.g. Adrian Peterson (2007).
  • Not included: careers that began before 1950 (e.g. Otto Graham, Bobby Layne, Y.A. Tittle, George Blanda), since their early seasons aren't in the data; stats from other leagues (AAFC, USFL, CFL); and the 1960s Playoff Bowl exhibitions. Games played can run a few low for 1950s–60s players, because the game logs only list games where a player recorded a stat.
